Governance, simply

Most people hear “governance” and think politics.

FRAME governance is much simpler.

Governance is just the rules for making decisions.

Imagine you have a shared family photo album. The rules might be:

  • Anyone can add photos.
  • Only Mom can delete photos.
  • If someone wants to delete the whole album, everyone has to agree.

That’s governance. Nothing more.

FRAME just lets you make those rules for anything.

Imagine a business. You might decide:

  • Employees can edit documents.
  • Managers can approve purchases.
  • The owner can change company settings.

Those are governance rules. FRAME remembers them and follows them automatically.

Or imagine you own a website. You could make a rule:

If the AI wants to deploy a new version…

↓

…two people must review it first.

Again—that’s governance.

It’s simply: who can do what, and under what conditions?

U.S. governance as an example

Remember: governance = the rules for making decisions.

The U.S. government is simply a very large governance system.

Level 1 — The People

The foundation is the people.

  • You vote for your mayor.
  • You vote for your governor.
  • You vote for the President.
  • You vote for members of Congress.

The people are the original source of authority.

Level 2 — The Constitution

Think of this as the master rulebook.

It says things like:

  • Everyone has free speech.
  • Government has limited powers.
  • There are three branches.
  • States have certain powers.
  • Rights cannot simply be taken away.

Everything underneath must follow it.

Level 3 — Federal Government

The Constitution creates three branches.

Executive Branch — President

Example decisions:

  • Signs bills into law
  • Commands the military
  • Runs federal agencies
  • Responds to disasters

Legislative Branch — Congress

Congress writes laws. Congress has two parts.

Senate — 100 senators

  • Approves treaties
  • Confirms Supreme Court judges
  • Votes on laws

House — 435 representatives

  • Creates spending bills
  • Votes on laws
  • Represents local districts

Judicial Branch — the courts

Someone argues: “This law violates free speech.” The courts decide. The Supreme Court has the final interpretation.

Federal Agencies

These carry out specific jobs:

  • IRS → taxes
  • FAA → aviation
  • NASA → space
  • EPA → environment
  • FBI → federal investigations
  • National Park Service → parks

Each agency has its own governance.

State Government

Each state has its own government. Arizona has a Governor, Legislature, and State Courts. They make rules for things like:

  • driver’s licenses
  • schools
  • state highways
  • state taxes
  • professional licenses

County Government — Pima County

The county governs things like:

  • Sheriff’s Office
  • County roads
  • Property records
  • Elections
  • Public health

City Government — Tucson

The city governs:

  • Police
  • Fire Department
  • Parks
  • Trash collection
  • Local streets
  • Zoning

Neighborhood — HOA

Sometimes even smaller. An HOA might set rules for:

  • Grass height
  • House colors
  • Community pool
  • Parking

That’s governance too.

The simplest way to think about government

Government isn’t one giant thing. It’s layers of governance, each responsible for different decisions.

Each layer has:

  • things it owns,
  • rules it follows,
  • decisions it can make,
  • and limits on its authority.

That’s exactly why “governance” is such a useful word for FRAME. It doesn’t mean politics—it simply means how decisions are made within a system. Whether that system is a country, a company, a family photo album, or a shared software project, governance defines who can decide what, under which conditions, and how those decisions are carried out.
